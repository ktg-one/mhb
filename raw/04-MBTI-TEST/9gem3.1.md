I am incredibly sorry that you are going through this. Losing a job, especially in a sudden and public way like a failed board presentation, is a deeply stressful and painful experience.

Please know that you are not the first person this has happened to, and you won't be the last. The integration of AI into the workplace is happening faster than most people can adapt to, and the learning curve is currently paved with situations exactly like yours.

To answer your question about what you should have done differently, it helps to look at this not to beat yourself up, but to build a bulletproof system for your next job.

Here is what you should do differently in the future when using AI in a professional setting:

### 1\. Change Your Core View of AI

**What likely happened:** You treated the AI like a search engine, a calculator, or a database of absolute facts.  
**What to do differently:** Treat AI like an eager, highly confident, but wildly inexperienced intern. AI models are "probabilistic"—they predict what word should come next based on patterns. They do not *know* facts, and they frequently "hallucinate" (invent) data, citations, and numbers that look completely realistic. You must assume everything the AI says is false until proven otherwise.

### 2\. Implement the "Trust but Verify" Rule

**What likely happened:** You took the data the AI generated and placed it directly into your presentation or analysis.  
**What to do differently:** Never let data go straight from an AI to a final stakeholder. You must establish a "Human-in-the-Loop" (HITL) workflow.

- If the AI gives you a statistic, find the primary source via a traditional Google search.
- If the AI runs a calculation, run a sample of those numbers yourself in Excel to ensure the formula is correct.
- If you cannot independently verify the data outside of the AI, you cannot use it.

### 3\. Establish a Peer Review Process

**What likely happened:** You worked in a silo between you and the AI, and the board was the first critical set of eyes on the data.  
**What to do differently:** Before high-stakes presentations, have a colleague or a direct manager review your data. Even if you don't mention AI, a second human looking at the numbers might say, "Wait, these numbers for Q3 don't make sense historically," catching the AI's hallucination before the board does.
