Quantum computing is a dual-use technology that creates a "cryptographic crisis" while simultaneously offering the next generation of "unbreakable" security.

### 1. The Immediate Threat: RSA and ECC Vulnerability

Current digital security relies on mathematical problems that are "hard" for classical computers but "easy" for quantum computers.

- **Shor's Algorithm:** A sufficiently powerful quantum computer can factor large prime numbers in polynomial time. This effectively breaks **RSA**, **Diffie-Hellman**, and **Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)**, which currently secure almost all web traffic, bank transactions, and government communications.
    
- **Grover's Algorithm:** This impacts symmetric encryption (like AES). While it doesn't "break" AES, it reduces its effective security. To maintain the same security level, key sizes must be doubled (e.g., moving from AES-128 to AES-256).
    

### 2. "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" (HNDL)

This is the most pressing risk in 2026. Adversaries are currently intercepting and storing encrypted sensitive data (intellectual property, state secrets). Even though they cannot read it today, they intend to decrypt it once a **Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC)** becomes available. If your data must remain secret for 10+ years, it is already at risk.

---

### 3. The Defense: Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)

To counter these threats, the industry is migrating to **Quantum-Resistant Algorithms**. These are classical math problems (like lattice-based cryptography) that even quantum computers struggle to solve.

- **NIST Standards (2026 Update):** The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology has finalized the first set of PQC standards (FIPS 203, 204, and 205).
    
- **Crypto-Agility:** Organizations are now forced to adopt "modular" security architectures that allow them to swap out compromised algorithms for new ones without rebuilding their entire infrastructure.
    

### 4. The Quantum Advantage: Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)

While quantum computers break old codes, quantum mechanics provides a way to create new ones.

- **Unbreakable Physics:** QKD uses the **No-Cloning Theorem**. If an eavesdropper attempts to intercept a quantum key (sent via photons), the act of observation alters the state of the particles, immediately alerting the sender and receiver.
    
- **Current State:** As of 2026, QKD is being deployed in satellite networks (e.g., ESA's QKDSat) and specialized fiber-optic links for high-security government and financial data.
