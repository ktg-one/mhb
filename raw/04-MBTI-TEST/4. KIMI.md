**The Setup Analysis**

- **$200K seed** in B2B SaaS for dentists: Extremely tight runway. Assuming 3 founders at minimal salary ($60K AUD each in Perth), that's $180K/year in personnel alone, plus AWS, compliance (dental has HIPAA-like requirements), and sales cycles. Runway: ~10-12 months maximum.
- **12 beta users**: Insufficient for meaningful validation. Dental SaaS requires practice management system integrations (Dentrix, Open Dental, Praktika), which are technically complex and sales-cycle heavy.
- **Perth launch**: Problematic market choice. Perth metro has ~2.5M population vs. Sydney/Melbourne's 5M+ each. Dental practice density is lower, and Perth's tech ecosystem is nascent—you're essentially building without a local customer development loop.

**The 18-Month Trajectory**

**Months 0-6: The False Positive**
- Beta users convert to paid at 30-40% (4-5 customers) due to founder relationships, not product-market fit.
- Founders burn 60% of capital on product development, underestimating dental compliance requirements (AHPRA, PIPEDA, state privacy laws).
- Attempt to raise pre-seed extension in Perth fails—local angels prefer mining tech or property. No institutional VC presence in Perth for SaaS.

**Months 6-12: The Crunch**
- Runway depletion forces salary cuts or second jobs. Technical debt from rapid MVP development surfaces—dental integrations break, causing churn among the 4-5 paid users.
- Sales cycles reveal structural problem: dental practices average 3-6 months decision time, require chairside trials, and decision-makers (practice owners) are time-poor and technologically conservative.
- Geographic isolation prevents cheap customer acquisition tactics that work in dense markets (in-person demos, local dental association events).

**Months 12-18: The Branch Point**

**Scenario A (70% probability): Shutdown**
- Capital exhausted. Unable to demonstrate $10K+ MRR required for seed extension. Founders disband—one returns to dentistry (if clinically qualified), others seek employment. IP likely abandoned or acqui-hired for nominal sum.

**Scenario B (25% probability): Zombie/Pivot**
- Survives on consulting revenue or white-labeling product to dental supply companies. MRR stagnates at $3-8K. Founders maintain day jobs, product becomes side project. No venture path remains.

**Scenario C (5% probability): Escape Velocity**
- One founder secures remote role in US/UK dental tech company, leverages network for distribution partnership. Raises $500K+ seed extension from Sydney/Melbourne VC on strength of 50+ customers and clear integration moat. Relocates headquarters east coast or operates distributed.

**Critical Failure Modes**

1. **Underestimating dental industry sales complexity**: B2B SaaS playbooks assume self-serve or inside sales. Dental requires field sales, which $200K cannot fund.
2. **Perch market isolation**: No critical mass of adjacent startups for talent, advice, or follow-on funding. Every investor conversation requires travel budget.
3. **Technical integration debt**: Dental software APIs are fragmented, poorly documented, and expensive. 12 beta users insufficient to justify development costs per integration.

**What Would Change the Outcome**

- Immediate relocation of at least one founder to Sydney/Melbourne or Brisbane for funding access and customer density.
- Pivot to "enabled services" model—using software to deliver actual dental services (teledentistry, patient financing) rather than practice management tools, improving unit economics.
- Strategic partnership with dental supply distributor (Henry Schein, SDI) in month 3-4, before runway criticality.

Without these interventions, the 18-month outcome is deterministic: capital exhaustion and team dissolution.
