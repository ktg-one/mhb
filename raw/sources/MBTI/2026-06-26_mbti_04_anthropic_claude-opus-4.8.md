## The uncomfortable base rate
Most seed-stage B2B SaaS startups don't reach a clean outcome in 18 months. The likeliest scenarios:

- **Still grinding, undercapitalized** (most common): $200K with 3 founders burns fast. If they're paying themselves even modest salaries, that money lasts ~12 months. They'll probably be raising again or hunting for revenue urgently by month 9-12.
- **Quiet pivot or shutdown**: 12 beta users is a starting signal, not traction. If those users don't convert to paying and refer others, this stalls.
- **Modest, real business**: Niche vertical SaaS for dentists *can* become a solid, profitable small company—not a rocket ship, but real.
- **Genuine breakout**: Possible, not probable.

## The things that actually determine the outcome
The Perth/dentist/$200K details matter less than these:

1. **Do the 12 beta users pay, and would they be angry if you took it away?** Free beta users tell you almost nothing. Paying, retained users tell you everything.
2. **Burn vs. runway.** $200K is very thin for 3 people. What's the monthly burn? That sets the real clock.
3. **Founder dynamics.** 3 founders is a common breakup number. Equity split, role clarity, and conflict resolution kill more startups than the market does.
4. **Market size in Perth.** Australia has ~16K dentists total; WA maybe ~1,500 practices. If they stay local-only, the ceiling is low. Plan to expand (national, then English-speaking markets) needs to exist early.
5. **Sales motion.** Dentists are busy, non-technical, and hard to reach. Customer acquisition cost can quietly destroy unit economics.

- Is it paid pilots or free?
- What's monthly net revenue retention?
- What's the burn rate?
- Why dentists, and why these founders—do they have a wedge (a founder who's a dentist or sold to them before)?
