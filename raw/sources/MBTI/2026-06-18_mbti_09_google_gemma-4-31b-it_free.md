### 1. Treat AI as a "Junior Intern," Not an Expert
The biggest mistake people make with LLMs (Large Language Models) is treating them like a search engine (like Google) or a calculator. AI does not "know" facts; it predicts the next likely word in a sentence based on patterns.

*   **The Mindset Shift:** You should treat AI output as a **first draft** written by an eager but unreliable intern. An intern might give you a great structure or a fast summary, but you would never send an intern's work to the board without checking every single number and claim.

### 2. Implement a "Verification Loop"
Never move data from an AI prompt directly into a final presentation. You should have implemented a three-step verification process:
*   **Step 1: The Prompt.** Use AI to organize, summarize, or brainstorm.
*   **Step 2: The Source Check.** For every data point the AI provided, you must find a primary source (a company report, a trusted database, a government website, or internal spreadsheets) to verify it.
*   **Step 3: The Audit.** If the AI cannot provide a verifiable source for a specific number, that number must be deleted.

### 3. Beware of "Hallucinations"
AI is designed to be helpful and fluent, which means it will often "hallucinate" (make things up) to satisfy your request rather than telling you "I don't know." 
*   **What to do differently:** When asking for data, specifically tell the AI: *"If you are unsure of a specific number or do not have the exact data, please state 'Data not found' rather than estimating."* Even then, you must still verify the results.

### 4. Use AI for Process, Not for Facts
AI is world-class at **processing** information, but mediocre at **providing** information.
*   **Wrong way:** "Give me the market growth rates for the logistics industry in 2023." (High risk of hallucination).
*   **Right way:** Paste a 50-page PDF of a market report into the AI and say, "Based *only* on the attached text, summarize the market growth rates for 2023 and cite the page number." (Low risk, because you provided the source).

### 5. Transparency and Disclosure
Depending on the company culture, using AI in secret can create a "trust gap." If the board finds out the data was AI-generated *after* it was proven wrong, it looks like negligence or deception.
*   **What to do differently:** Be transparent about your workflow. "I used AI to help synthesize this data, and then I cross-referenced it with [Source X] and [Source Y] to ensure accuracy." This shows you are using modern tools efficiently but maintaining professional rigor.
