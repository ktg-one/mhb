- **B2B SaaS for dentists**

Assuming the founders are full-time and frugal, $200K gives them maybe **9–15 months of runway**, depending on whether they pay themselves, use contractors, or spend on sales.

Perth is a useful launch market: small enough to build reputation, relationship-driven, and geographically contained. But dental practices are fragmented, busy, and often conservative about software.

# Likely 18-month outcome

By month 18, a realistic outcome might be:

- **40–100 paying clinics**
- **$8K–$40K MRR**, depending on pricing
- **Churn visible but manageable**
- **One or two clear customer segments emerging**

But if they are charging only $50–$100/month, they may have customers but not a business.

# What probably happens by phase

## Months 0–3: Beta feedback and product reality

The 12 beta users give encouraging feedback, but much of it is misleading.

Dentists may say:

> “This is great, I’d definitely use it.”

But once asked to pay, integrate it into workflow, train staff, or change daily habits, enthusiasm drops.

The founders discover that the real users may not be the dentists themselves but:

- practice managers
- reception staff
- hygienists
- treatment coordinators
- owners of multi-chair clinics

This is important because the buyer, user, and beneficiary may be different people.

The product likely needs tightening around one urgent workflow, such as:

- patient recall
- appointment no-shows
- treatment plan follow-up
- patient communication
- payment plans
- compliance/documentation
- reputation management
- inventory
- staff rostering
- chair utilisation
- insurance/claims support

If the product is too broad — “practice management platform for dentists” — they struggle. Dental clinics already have practice management systems and do not want to rip them out.

The winning wedge is probably something that sits alongside existing systems and creates obvious ROI.

## Months 3–6: First paying customers

They convert some beta users.

A typical conversion might be:

- 12 beta users
- 6–9 remain active
- 3–6 become paying customers quickly
- others ask to “revisit later”

They start selling manually through:

- Perth dental networks
- personal referrals
- dental associations
- local events
- cold outreach
- founder visits to clinics
- LinkedIn
- partnerships with consultants, accountants, or equipment suppliers

They learn that dentists are hard to reach during working hours. Receptionists gatekeep. Email response rates are poor. Demos need to be short and practical.

If the software clearly increases revenue or reduces admin, they can sell. If it is merely “nice to have,” sales cycles drag.

By month 6, a reasonable target is:

- **10–25 paying clinics**
- **$2K–$10K MRR**
- **strong qualitative learning**
- **a sharper ICP**
