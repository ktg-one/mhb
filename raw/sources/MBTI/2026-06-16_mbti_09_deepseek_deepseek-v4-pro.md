## What you should have done differently — a practical checklist

### 1. Never present an AI-generated number without a source you’ve personally verified.
LLMs (even the best ones) hallucinate facts, statistics, dates, and figures—and do so with alarming confidence. A number that sounds perfectly plausible may be entirely fabricated. Before it goes anywhere near a deck:
- Find the original source (report, database, official site) yourself.
- If the AI can’t give you a specific, retrievable source, mark it “unverified” and do not use it until you have ground truth.
- Run a sanity check: “Does this number make sense given what I know about our business?”

### 2. Distinguish between *generating ideas* and *delivering facts*.
Using AI to brainstorm slide titles, suggest a narrative flow, or rephrase a bullet point is fine. But the moment it supplies data, quotes, or specific performance figures, switch from “assistant” mode to “fact-checker” mode. If you’re not willing to bet your job on a fact’s accuracy, don’t put it in front of leadership.
