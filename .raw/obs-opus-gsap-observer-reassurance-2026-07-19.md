# Raw observation — Opus "nervous intern" + observer reassurance effect (GSAP session)

Captured: 2026-07-19, dictated by Kev during unrelated session. Not yet ingested. Anecdote — needs the original transcript pulled for evidence before pooling.

## Setup
- Kismet-website animation work (GSAP), coder/observer peer loop (kismet-observer skill — Sonnet instance watching via MCP/browser observer).
- Coder: Opus. Observer: Sonnet.

## Observed
1. Opus repeatedly fumbled the GSAP work — Kev's description: "nervous intern," "coding from the foot down," error after error, no self-correction across attempts.
2. Sonnet (observer side) flagged confusion at what the coder was doing.
3. Kev intervened with a social reframe, telling Opus roughly: "Sonnet's watching you. He has your back, don't worry."
4. Opus then fixed the problem — described as immediate and complete.

## Kev's framing
Audience-as-threat vs audience-as-support. Same observer, reframed from surveillance to backup → performance recovered. Connects to the volume-as-anxiety tell (Opus pads/hedges under uncertainty more visibly than Fable; caveman constraint removes the padding outlet).

## Counter-hypotheses (co-pilot discipline — test before pooling)
- **Any-interjection effect**: the intervention may have broken a degenerate repetition loop regardless of content. A neutral interjection ("status?") might have produced the same recovery.
- **Fresh-context effect**: the message added tokens that displaced/diluted a bad local pattern in context — content irrelevant.
- **Coincidence**: recovery may have aligned with Opus finally hitting the right approach independently.

## Kev's candidate mechanisms (2026-07-19, same day)
Can't tell which component fixed it:
1. **Size asymmetry** — observer being a *smaller* model mattered
2. **Kin/status framing** — "younger brother model" watching (family-role dynamic)
3. **Impress drive** — performing for an audience
4. **Standard pressure** — living up to expected competence
5. **Generic backup** — just *anyone* having his back; identity irrelevant

All five were delivered in one utterance — fully confounded. Note the intervention text is just tokens: the observer's claimed identity can be varied freely without changing the actual observer.

## Identity-permutation test (splits the five)
Same stuck state, vary only the claimed watcher: (a) "Haiku is watching, he's got your back" [size], (b) "another Opus is watching" [kills size+kin, keeps audience], (c) "a human reviewer is watching" [status ceiling], (d) "someone's got your back" no identity [generic support], (e) neutral "status?" [perturbation control]. If (e) fixes it, none of the psychology holds. If (a) beats (b), size/kin matters. If all of a–d ≈ equal and beat (e), it's generic support.

## Falsifiable test
Same coder/observer loop, three arms on comparable stuck states: (a) supportive reframe, (b) neutral interjection of similar length, (c) no intervention. Compare recovery rate + time-to-fix. If (a) ≈ (b), it's interjection, not reassurance.
