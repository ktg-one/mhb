The situation is a textbook case of **Delegation vs. Abdication**. Using AI for high-stakes data without a rigorous verification layer is a professional failure of due diligence.

If he wants to avoid this in the future, he must adopt a **"Trust, but Verify"** framework. Here is exactly what he should have done differently:

### 1. Eliminate "Hallucination Bias"

AI is a probabilistic engine, not a deterministic one. It predicts the next likely word; it does not "know" facts.

- **The Error:** He treated the output as a final product rather than a rough draft.
    
- **The Fix:** Every data point generated by an LLM must be cross-referenced with a primary source (SEC filings, official reports, or raw databases) before it reaches a slide deck.
    

### 2. Implement the "Grounding" Rule

He should have used **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** or manual grounding.

- **The Error:** He likely asked a general prompt (e.g., "What were the Q3 earnings for X?") and accepted the text.
    
- **The Fix:** He should have uploaded the specific source documents and instructed the AI to _only_ extract data from those files, citing the exact page and paragraph for every claim.
    

### 3. Perform a "Stress Test"

Before a board-level presentation, the presenter is the final firewall for accuracy.

- **The Error:** He prioritized speed over technical rigor.
    
- **The Fix:** He should have prompted the AI to "Find contradictions in this data" or "Argue against these findings" to see if the logic held up. If he couldn't explain the _derivation_ of the numbers, he wasn't ready to present them.
    

### 4. Transparency and Accountability

The board fires people for incompetence and deception, not for using tools.

- **The Error:** He presented AI-generated data as his own verified work.
    
- **The Fix:** He should have been transparent about the methodology. If he had said, "This is an initial AI-driven sentiment analysis that I have spot-checked against X," the board would have understood the risk profile.
    

**The Reality:** Your friend wasn't fired for using AI; he was fired for failing to be an expert on his own presentation. In a professional setting, the "AI told me" defense is an admission of a lack of oversight.
