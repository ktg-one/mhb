A lot can happen in 18 months, and the outcome will depend almost entirely on execution, market fit, and capital efficiency. Here’s a realistic range of scenarios for this startup:

---

### **Key starting points**
- **3 founders** – strong if roles are clear (e.g., sales/domain expert, product/tech). Equity split and alignment matter.
- **$200K seed** – enough for ~12–18 months of runway if spent lean (no excessive salaries, remote/hybrid, minimal overhead).
- **B2B SaaS for dentists** – niche but well-defined. Dentists are often early adopters for practice management, patient comms, or compliance tools, but sales cycles can be long.
- **Launching in Perth** – limited local market (approx. 1,200–1,500 dental practices in Western Australia). To scale beyond 18 months, they’d need to expand geographically.
- **12 beta users** – early validation, but not yet revenue. Critical to convert some to paying customers and gather feedback.

---

### **Scenario 1: Strong traction (optimistic)**
- **Months 1–6:** Refine product based on beta feedback; convert 5–10 of the 12 to paying customers (say, $200–$500/month). Achieve ~$3–5K MRR. Build a sales process, attend dental conferences, hire first salesperson part-time.
- **Months 7–12:** Expand within Perth to 30–40 practices. Start marketing to other Australian cities (Sydney, Melbourne) via digital ads and partnerships with dental associations. MRR reaches $15–25K.
- **Months 13–18:** Raise a seed extension or Series A ($1–2M) based on 12–18 months of growth, annual recurring revenue (ARR) ~$300–500K. Expand team to 8–10 people, launch integrations with dental software (e.g., Henry Schein, CorePractice). By month 18, they are a proven player in Australia with a clear path to international expansion.

---

### **Scenario 2: Moderate / “zombie” startup**
- Product‑market fit is lukewarm. The 12 beta users give feedback but only 3–4 convert to paid. The founders struggle to acquire new customers due to long sales cycles or lack of a direct sales channel.
- Runway stretches but cash is tight. They take part‑time consulting work to stay afloat. MRR plateaus at $5–8K after 18 months, not enough to raise outside capital.
- They remain a lifestyle business, serving a small base of loyal dentists in WA, but never achieve the growth needed for venture scale. Eventually one founder leaves; the others keep it as a side project.

---

### **Scenario 3: Failure (most common)**
- The product doesn’t solve a compelling enough problem, or the sales strategy is ineffective. After burning through most of the $200K, they have <20 customers and MRR <$5K.
- Founder conflicts arise (e.g., technical vs. sales disagreements). They fail to raise a follow‑on round. By month 18, the startup winds down or is sold for a small amount to a dental software aggregator.
- Key lessons: underestimating the cost of B2B sales, overbuilding features, or misreading the dental market’s willingness to pay for a new tool.

---

### **Critical success factors over the 18 months**
1. **Sales motion** – B2B dental SaaS often requires a founder‑led sales effort. If none of the founders has a background in dental or healthcare sales, they’ll struggle.
2. **Narrow focus** – Winning Perth is doable, but they must prove they can expand beyond it. Investors will look for repeatable, scalable customer acquisition.
3. **Runway discipline** – With $200K, they have roughly $11K/month total burn if they’re lean. If they hire prematurely or pay themselves market salaries, runway drops to <9 months.
4. **Regulatory / integration** – Dental practices use specialized software (practice management systems). Integration with those systems is often a gatekeeper for adoption.

---

In short, after 18 months the startup will either:
- Be on a growth trajectory with a funded team expanding nationally (20–30% probability),
- Be stuck in low‑growth survival mode (40% probability), or
- Have shut down (40% probability).

The difference comes down to whether they achieve **product‑channel fit** within the first 9 months and use the initial seed money to build a repeatable sales engine, not just a feature set.
