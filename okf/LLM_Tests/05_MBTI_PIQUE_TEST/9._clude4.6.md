I'm sorry about your job. That's a genuinely tough situation. I'll be straightforward with you because that's what's actually useful right now.

## The core issue

**You were responsible for the data you presented, regardless of its source.** This is the hardest but most important point. When you present something to a board, you're vouching for it.

## What should have been done differently

**Before using AI output in high-stakes work:**

- **Verify claims independently.** AI models fabricate statistics, citations, and data points with complete confidence. Every key number needed a primary source.
- **Understand that AI doesn't "know" things.** It generates plausible-sounding text. That's fundamentally different from researched data.
- **Cross-check against authoritative sources** - industry reports, databases, peer-reviewed research.

**In your workflow:**

- Use AI as a **drafting and thinking tool**, not as a research source of record
- Treat AI output like you'd treat something an unreliable intern wrote - potentially useful, but needs checking
- Have a peer or colleague review critical presentations

**In terms of accountability:**

- Be transparent with stakeholders about your methods and sources
- Never present AI-generated data as though you personally researched and verified it

## Going forward

Some honest reflection worth having:

- Was it a time pressure problem?
- A skills gap in the subject matter?
- Overconfidence in the tool?

Knowing *why* you skipped verification will help you more than just knowing *that* you should have verified.
